The design loop

Each choice sets up the next one

Whether a crop-protection technology is sustainable is decided less by what it kills than by what it leaves behind. All five of our design axes came out of that question — and they are not five independent choices but a chain, where each decision becomes the condition for the next.

Zero Hunger
Persistence · Secondary claim

One inoculation, protection that lasts the season

01 · The goal
SDG 2 asks for an end to hunger — reliable access to enough safe food for everyone. Target 2.4 names the means: resilient farming practices that keep producing.
02 · The problem
Agriculture loses a substantial share of what it grows to pests and pathogens every year. Losing less is a lighter environmental option than clearing more land — but conventional control leaves gaps between spray cycles, and losses accumulate in exactly those gaps.
03 · What we do
P. putida KT2440 colonises the rhizosphere instead of washing off. The cassette is integrated into the chromosome, so protection is inherited without antibiotic selection — and one strain answers both Bradysia larvae and Fusarium.
04 · Why it matters
When one inoculation covers a season, both the number of re-applications and the losses between them fall. Food security is not only growing more — it is keeping more of what already grew.

Limit — we have no field-level yield data yet. For now this is a designed intention.

Life on Land
Selectivity · Primary claim

Narrow in space, narrow in spectrum

01 · The goal
SDG 15 protects and restores terrestrial ecosystems; target 15.5 calls for halting biodiversity loss. Farmland is not outside that — it is one of the ecosystems in question.
02 · The problem
A pesticide's reach is usually far wider than the distribution of the organism it is aimed at. Broad-spectrum products do not distinguish target from bystander: decomposers, pollinators and beneficial soil microbes thin out together, and that loss comes back as lost fertility.
03 · What we do
Three independent limits: SA sensing confines activity to millimetres around the wound; Cry4Aa acts through dipteran midgut receptors; the antifungal effector travels by contact-dependent T6SS, so nothing diffuses away. Co-culture with beneficial soil bacteria tests for antagonism.
04 · Why it matters
Narrowing where control acts is the same thing as leaving the soil food web intact. That is the condition for using farmland and maintaining it at once.

Limit — the co-culture work is preliminary, and we have not yet assessed the effect on the whole soil community.

Good Health and Well-being
Precision · Primary claim

Nothing toxic is in the sprayer

01 · The goal
SDG 3 is healthy lives at every age. Target 3.9 is specific about chemicals: reduce the illness and death caused by hazardous substances and contamination.
02 · The problem
A pesticide is built to be toxic — that is the point of it. Pesticide poisoning is an occupational illness reported across many regions, and exposure concentrates at mixing and spraying, where the product is at its most concentrated and the worker stands closest to it. Residue reaches the consumer later.
03 · What we do
The nahR/Pm cascade keeps the toxin genes silent until salicylic acid reports damage. What goes into the tank is a living cell carrying instructions — the toxin is made at the root, after the signal, and only there.
04 · Why it matters
The toxin is absent from the moment of greatest exposure. And with no synthetic active ingredient applied, there is no residue for the consumer to carry home.

Limit — we have not yet assessed inhalation or allergenic exposure to the live cells themselves.

Climate Action
Toxin platform · Secondary claim

The chassis stays, the cargo swaps

01 · The goal
SDG 13 covers both halves of the climate response — cutting emissions, and adapting to what is already changing. Target 13.1 is the second half: strengthening adaptive capacity.
02 · The problem
As temperatures rise, the range a pest can occupy widens and the number of generations per season grows, so the list of things to control keeps changing. Developing and registering a new chemical typically takes years and does not move at that speed.
03 · What we do
LARD3 exports whatever is fused to it, so Cry4Aa can be exchanged for another insecticidal protein; the T6SS effector is a slot on the same logic. The SA sensor that decides when never changes when we change what.
04 · Why it matters
Our climate claim is adaptation speed, not carbon. Retargeting becomes a cloning step, which is what lets the technology follow a pest map that will not stop moving.

Limit — we have not yet swapped in a different toxin module. For now this is a property of the design.

Responsible Consumption and Production
Formulation & biosafety · Primary claim

Containable to make, stable to ship

01 · The goal
SDG 12 asks that production and consumption be sustainable across the whole life cycle. Target 12.4 names the sound management of chemicals and wastes throughout it.
02 · The problem
What stops a biopesticide on the way out of the lab is usually not efficacy but containment and distribution: a plasmid can be passed to soil bacteria by conjugation, production needs antibiotic selection, and live cells need a cold chain.
03 · What we do
Moving the circuit to a defined chromosomal landing pad removes the mobilisable element — and the conjugative route with it. An integrated copy needs no selection marker, and a lyophilised powder needs no refrigeration; viability after drying is confirmed by re-plating.
04 · Why it matters
Containment, marker-free production and a dry format are one decision, not three. Together they make a product that is lighter to distribute and far easier to defend in front of a regulator.

Limit — what this closes is the conjugative route, not horizontal gene transfer in general.
